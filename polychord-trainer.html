How a Polychord / slash chord is built

A Polychord / slash chord — written / (e.g. C/) — is a 7-note chord: a major triad (Lesson 8) rooted a whole step above the bass note, stacked over a dominant 7th chord (Lesson 13) built on that bass note — the app’s reference voicing for "X/Y" slash-chord notation. Every / chord — in any of the 12 keys — is built the exact same way, by counting semitones to the right from a root note:

  1. The starting note — this is the bass note, the "denominator" of the slash chord.
  2. Build a plain dominant 7th chord (Lesson 13) on this bass note: +4 (major 3rd, 4 semitones), +7 (perfect 5th), +10 (minor 7th) — that is the "lower" chord.
  3. Then count 2 semitones up from the root — this is the root of the "upper" triad stacked on top.
  4. Build a plain major triad (Lesson 8) on that note, a whole step above the bass: +4 more semitones (6 from the root) for its 3rd, and +3 more (9 from the root) for its 5th.
  5. The combined 7-note sound — a major triad rooted a whole step above the bass, played over a dominant 7th chord — is this app’s reference polychord, transposable to any of the 12 keys.
Example — C/: start on C, count 2 semitones right to reach D, count 2 semitones more to reach E, count 2 semitones more to reach F♯, count 1 semitone more to reach G, count 2 semitones more to reach A, count 1 semitone more to reach B♭. C‑D‑E‑F♯‑G‑A‑B♭ is the C/ chord.
A polychord is really two chords at once — the notation "D/C7" literally means "play a D major triad over a C7 chord." This app fixes that relationship (a major triad a whole step above the bass, over a dominant 7th on the bass) so it can transpose to all 12 keys as one consistent "chord type," the way modern gospel reharmonizations often use it.

This MIDI exercise plays all 12 polychords, one after another, moving around the circle of fourths — each chord's root sits a perfect 4th (5 semitones) above the last one: C → F → B♭ → E♭ → A♭ → D♭ → G♭/F♯ → B → E → A → D → G → back to C. Every chord is played as a full 7-note Polychord / slash chord (root (bass), upper triad root (+2), major 3rd, upper triad 3rd (+6), perfect 5th, upper triad 5th (+9), minor 7th together).
